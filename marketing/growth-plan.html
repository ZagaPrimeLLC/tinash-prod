Tinash Homecare Services · Growth plan

Getting found, chosen and called back in New Jersey

A 90-day plan for SEO, Google ranking, social media, publicity and a small ad budget. It is built from research on how NJ home care and DDD agencies actually win clients, plus an audit of tinashhomecareservices.com.

Prepared 6 October 2026 · Covers 6 Oct 2026 to 4 Jan 2027 · Counties: Essex, Union, Middlesex, Somerset, Morris, Hudson (plus Monmouth for hiring)

The short version

Tinash is barely visible online, and that is the first thing to fix

Tinash has a verified Google Business Profile (since March 2024) with 4 reviews, the newest from February 2025, none answered. Google has indexed 8 pages of the site, and the site got 18 clicks from Google search in the last three months. The profile still lists the old phone number, says "open 24 hours", and its service area includes "United States" and Sussex County instead of the six counties Tinash serves. Competitors in Essex and Morris show 70 to 140 reviews each.

Three priorities, in this order

  1. Be findable and trusted (weeks 1–2). Google Business Profile, Search Console, the NJ DDD Provider Search listing, consistent name/address/phone everywhere, and fixes to the site's speed and setup.
  2. Build the two referral engines (weeks 3–8). Reviews from happy families, and relationships with the people who send clients: DDD support coordinators, hospital and rehab discharge planners, county aging offices.
  3. Add reach (weeks 6–13). Town and county pages, a steady content and social rhythm, local press, and a small Google Search ads test once tracking works.

Ads come last on purpose. Paid clicks for in-home care cost about $11 each, and sending them to a business with no reviews and no Google listing wastes most of the money.

Speed of response decides a lot of this

Across senior care, only 53% of inbound calls reach a person, and 60% of providers never ask the caller to book an assessment (Invoca 2026). Leads contacted within 5 minutes are far more likely to convert than those called back after 30. Every website lead now emails phane@ instantly; the office needs a rule for who calls back and how fast.

Three audiences, three paths

Market each service line where its families actually look

Private-pay home care

Adult children 45–65 arranging care for a parent, and seniors themselves.

  • Google Maps and search (Business Profile, reviews, county pages)
  • Discharge planners, rehabs, senior centers, elder law attorneys
  • Facebook and YouTube (74% and 85% of 50–64 year olds use them)
  • Google Search ads in month 2

NJ DDD services

Families of adults 21+ on the Community Care or Supports Program.

  • Support coordination agencies (visits, a one-page provider sheet)
  • DDD Provider Search, NJ 211, county ResourceNet directories
  • Transition fairs, SPAN, Arc of NJ chapters, school districts
  • Plain-language guides on the website (budgets, IS vs CBS)

GUIDE dementia program

Families of someone with dementia on traditional Medicare.

  • Physicians, memory clinics, Alzheimer's Association NJ support groups
  • A clear eligibility checker on the GUIDE page
  • Wording approved by PocketRN (the program participant)
  • November is Alzheimer's Awareness Month: the natural launch

Comfort Keepers of Montclair already markets a PocketRN GUIDE partnership across Essex, Union and Morris, so Tinash will not be the only one. Tinash can stand out by being clearer about who qualifies (traditional Medicare Parts A and B only, not Medicare Advantage) and by its DDD experience.

Ads

Recommendation: a small Google Search test, nothing else yet

Google Search

Start in late November · $750 / month

Keywords such as "home care Montclair NJ", "in-home senior care Essex County", "DDD respite provider NJ", "dementia care at home NJ". Calls and assessments only. Expect about 65–100 clicks and 5–10 inquiries a month to start.

Google Local Services Ads

Check eligibility first

Pay per lead rather than per click, and shows a "Google screened" badge. It is unconfirmed whether home care qualifies in NJ, so check in the sign-up tool before spending.

Meta (Facebook / Instagram)

Recruiting only, for now

Health advertisers face strict targeting limits on Meta. Use it for DSP and caregiver job ads (Employment category), and boost the best organic family posts lightly.

Rules to follow: no remarketing or audiences based on health conditions or disability (Google bans it, including targeting caregivers of people with a condition), and no ad tracking pixels on intake forms or the CRM. One new private-pay client can cover months of this budget, so the test pays off if it converts even one or two families a month.

Never miss a call

A Tinash phone receptionist, built in-house on a Raspberry Pi 5

Only about half of calls to senior-care providers reach a person, and a missed call usually goes to the next agency on the list. Hosted AI receptionists such as Quo's agent charge a monthly fee per seat or per call. Tinash can run its own on a Raspberry Pi 5 in the office, using free, open-source software. The one cost that cannot be avoided is a phone line that can talk to it, about $1–3 a month for a number plus about half a cent to one cent per minute.

What callers get

  • Answered on the first ring, after hours and when the office is busy
  • Told it is Tinash's virtual assistant and that the call is noted
  • Plain answers on services, counties, hours, DDD and GUIDE basics
  • Transfer to a person during office hours; 911 advice for emergencies

What it writes down

  • Caller name and callback number
  • Who needs care, which service, town or county
  • How it would be paid: private, DDD budget, Medicare
  • How urgent, best time to call back, a short summary

Where notes go

  • Saved as an inquiry in the CRM inbox, marked "Phone assistant"
  • That triggers the same instant email to phane@ as website forms
  • Job seekers are logged as caregiver inquiries

Limits to plan around

  • Speed. In tests the assistant starts speaking about 3–4 seconds after the caller stops, most of it speech recognition. On the Pi this may be a little slower.
  • Privacy. Calls contain health details, and Claude processes the conversation. Sign Anthropic's business associate agreement (BAA) before real patient calls; until then, test calls only. Transcripts stay on the Pi and are deleted after 30 days.
  • Fallback. If the Pi is off or the internet is down, the carrier sends calls to voicemail or a staff cell phone.

Steps

  1. Built and tested on a Mac (done 7 October). Claude runs the calls; test calls pass, including emergencies, price questions and early hang-ups. Next: the office reviews the script and fact sheet and tries it by microphone, then sign up for Telnyx, set up the tunnel, and move it to the Pi.
  2. After hours only (2 weeks). Forward calls only outside office hours. Review every summary with the office and tune the script.
  3. Overflow (from early December). Also answer daytime calls nobody picks up within 4 rings.
Guardrails

Compliance points that shape the marketing

  • Reviews and HIPAA. A NJ practice paid $30,000 for revealing patient details in review replies. Never confirm someone is a client in a reply. Ask for reviews only from families who agree.
  • GUIDE wording. Only traditional Medicare (Parts A and B, Medicare primary) qualifies, not Medicare Advantage, PACE, hospice or long-term nursing home residents. No gifts or incentives to enroll. Families keep free choice of providers. Have PocketRN approve flyers and web copy, and do not use the Medicare logo.
  • DDD. Present Tinash as an option for the family to choose. Support coordinators help narrow the list but do not choose for the family.
  • Licensing. Show the Health Care Service Firm registration number on the site once renewed.
